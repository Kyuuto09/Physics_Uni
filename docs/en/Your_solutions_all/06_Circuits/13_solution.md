# Problem 13: Transformer Ratio

### 1. Problem Statement

A transformer is used to step down the voltage from $120\text{ V}$ AC to $9.0\text{ V}$ AC. If the primary coil has $400\text{ turns}$, how many turns must the secondary coil have?

---

### 2. Solution and Explanation

**Concept Intuition:**
Building directly on the previous problem, the core principle of an ideal transformer is that the ratio of the voltages is exactly equal to the ratio of the wire turns. 

If you want the voltage to drop to a tiny fraction of its original value, you simply ensure the secondary coil has that exact same tiny fraction of wire loops compared to the primary coil. 

#### Step 1: Set up the Transformer Equation
The standard equation relates the secondary and primary sides:
$$\frac{V_s}{V_p} = \frac{N_s}{N_p}$$

Where:
*   $V_s = 9.0\text{ V}$ (Secondary Voltage)
*   $V_p = 120\text{ V}$ (Primary Voltage)
*   $N_p = 400\text{ turns}$ (Primary Turns)

#### Step 2: Solve for the Secondary Turns
We can rearrange the equation to isolate the number of secondary turns ($N_s$):
$$N_s = N_p \cdot \left( \frac{V_s}{V_p} \right)$$

Substitute the known values into the equation:
$$N_s = 400 \cdot \left( \frac{9.0}{120} \right)$$
$$N_s = 400 \cdot \left( \frac{3}{40} \right)$$
$$N_s = 10 \cdot 3$$
$$N_s = 30\text{ turns}$$

---

### 3. Final Answer

*   **Number of Secondary Turns:** $30\text{ turns}$
