# Problem 10: Lorentz Force acting on Wire

### 1. Problem Statement

A straight wire 2.0 m long carries a current of 10 A. It is placed in a uniform magnetic field of B = 0.5 T. Calculate the magnetic force on the wire if the angle between the wire and the magnetic field is:
a) $90^\circ$
b) $45^\circ$
c) $0^\circ$

---

### 2. Solution and Explanation

**Concept Intuition:**
This is an incredibly straightforward problem! Rather than tracking exactly one tiny isolated flying proton, we logically look at an entirely solid metal wire crammed with billions of heavily moving electrons representing a steady solid current ($I$).
The general formula for the macroscopic magnetic force strictly on a straight length of current-carrying wire is precisely mathematically derived tightly from the individual identical Lorentz force:
$$F = I L B \sin(\theta)$$

Where:
- $I$ is the physical current identically replacing velocity $v$.
- $L$ is the completely specific length of wire sitting completely inside the magnetic field.
- $B$ is the heavily uniform magnetic field intensity.
- $\theta$ is the precise incoming angle rigidly locked specifically between the direction of electrical current perfectly matching the wire's length and the magnetic field itself.

Let's efficiently pre-calculate the massive core multiplication completely independently of the angle!
$$I \cdot L \cdot B = (10 \text{ A}) \cdot (2.0 \text{ m}) \cdot (0.5 \text{ T})$$
$$I \cdot L \cdot B = 10 \text{ N}$$

#### Part A: Exactly Perpendicular ($90^\circ$)
When perfectly completely perpendicular, mathematically $\sin(90^\circ) = 1$. The rigid wire experiences the absolute extreme maximum possible side-pushing force precisely flawlessly.
$$F = (10 \text{ N}) \cdot \sin(90^\circ)$$
$$F = 10 \cdot 1 = 10 \text{ N}$$

#### Part B: Angled Exactly at $45^\circ$
When carefully shifted to completely $45^\circ$, some of the current is completely violently fighting pushing partially "against" the magnetic field, rigidly mathematically decreasing the exact absolute total force.
$$F = (10 \text{ N}) \cdot \sin(45^\circ)$$
$$F = 10 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$$
$$F = 5\sqrt{2} \text{ N} \approx 7.07 \text{ N}$$

#### Part C: Exactly Parallel ($0^\circ$)
If the heavily-crammed wire points rigidly perfectly forward in the exact same distinct direction as the uniform magnetic field arrows ($\theta = 0^\circ$ or $180^\circ$ respectively), $\sin(0^\circ) = 0$. 
The incredibly powerful magnetic field perfectly ignores anything flawlessly completely flying directly perfectly linearly alongside it!
$$F = (10 \text{ N}) \cdot \sin(0^\circ)$$
$$F = 10 \cdot 0 = 0 \text{ N}$$

---

### 3. Final Answers

- **a) For $90^\circ$:** $10 \text{ N}$
- **b) For $45^\circ$:** $5\sqrt{2} \text{ N}$ (or $\approx 7.07 \text{ N}$)
- **c) For $0^\circ$:** $0 \text{ N}$
