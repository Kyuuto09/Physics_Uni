# Problem 8: Lorentz Force

### 1. Problem Statement

A charged particle with charge $q = 2 \times 10^{-19}$ C and mass $m = 4 \times 10^{-27}$ kg enters a magnetic field of $B = 0.5$ T at a speed of $v = 10^6$ m/s perpendicular to the field. What is the magnitude of the Lorentz force acting on the particle?

---

### 2. Solution and Explanation

**Concept Intuition:**
The Lorentz force is the fundamental interaction describing exactly how moving purely charged particles react physically when passing through a powerful magnetic field. 

Unlike gravitation or electrostatic repulsion where things just push or pull perfectly straight towards each other, the magnetic Lorentz Force has a phenomenally weird and purely unique quirk: **it only pushes horizontally sideways**. The magnetic field completely ignores stationary particles. It only attacks particles that act bravely enough to *move* through it, violently pushing them directly perpendicular to whatever direction they are attempting to travel!

#### Part A: Application of the Lorentz Formula

The full mathematical magnitude of the exact magnetic force on a moving charge is flawlessly governed by the equation:
$$F_L = |q|vB \sin(\theta)$$

Where:
- $q$ is the complete magnitude of the specific charge.
- $v$ is the precise velocity (speed).
- $B$ is the immense strength of the uniform magnetic field.
- $\theta$ is the precise incoming angle mathematically locked strictly between the velocity vector and the magnetic field vector.

#### Part B: The Perpendicular Effect

The problem heavily emphasizes that the incredibly fast particle specifically enters **perpendicular** to the magnetic field.
Mathematically, "perpendicular" perfectly translates to a precise $90^\circ$ angle.
$$\theta = 90^\circ \implies \sin(90^\circ) = 1$$

Therefore, the particle will experience exactly the absolute **maximum possible** magnetic force:
$$F_L = |q|vB$$

#### Part C: Complete Numerical Calculation

We flawlessly substitute all precisely provided numbers carefully into our completely optimized equation:
- $q = 2 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$
- $v = 10^6 \text{ m/s}$
- $B = 0.5 \text{ T}$

$$F_L = (2 \times 10^{-19}) \cdot (10^6) \cdot (0.5)$$

Using immensely simple scientific exponent combination:
$$F_L = (2 \cdot 0.5) \times (10^{-19} \cdot 10^6)$$
$$F_L = 1 \times 10^{-13} \text{ N}$$
$$F_L = 10^{-13} \text{ N}$$

*(Note: The particle's given mass $m = 4 \times 10^{-27} \text{ kg}$ is completely mathematically irrelevant for finding the raw incoming force. It would only be necessary if a follow-up question specifically asked how fast the force accelerates the particle using $F=ma$.)*

---

### 3. Final Answers

- **Magnitude of Lorentz Force ($F_L$):** precisely $1.0 \times 10^{-13} \text{ N}$.
