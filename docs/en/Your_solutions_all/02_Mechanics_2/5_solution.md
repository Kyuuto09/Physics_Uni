# Problem 5: Inelastic Collision

### 1. Problem Statement

A 70 kg runner moving at $3 \text{ m/s}$ jumps onto a 140 kg stationary cart. What is the final speed of the cart with the runner? Is kinetic energy conserved in this collision? Explain.

---

### 2. Solution and Explanation

**Concept Intuition:**
When two objects collide and stick together (like a runner jumping onto a cart), they form a single combined mass. This is called a _perfectly inelastic collision_.
In every collision, **momentum is always conserved**. However, **kinetic energy is not conserved** when objects stick together, because some of the movement energy is transformed into other forms of energy (like sound, the physical impact, and heat from friction).

#### Step 1: Find the final speed (Conservation of Momentum)

The total momentum before the jump must equal the total momentum after the jump.
$$P_{before} = P_{after}$$
$$m_1v_1 + m_2v_2 = (m_1 + m_2)v_{final}$$

We know the mass of the runner ($m_1 = 70 \text{ kg}$) and their speed ($v_1 = 3 \text{ m/s}$).
The cart ($m_2 = 140 \text{ kg}$) is stationary, so its initial speed ($v_2$) is $0$.

Let's plug in the numbers:
$$(70 \cdot 3) + (140 \cdot 0) = (70 + 140) \cdot v_{final}$$
$$210 + 0 = 210 \cdot v_{final}$$
$$210 = 210 \cdot v_{final}$$

Divide both sides by $210$ to find the final velocity:
$$v_{final} = \frac{210}{210}$$
$$v_{final} = 1 \text{ m/s}$$

_The runner and the cart move together at a final speed of 1 m/s._

#### Step 2: Check for Conservation of Kinetic Energy

To prove whether kinetic energy is conserved, we must calculate the total kinetic energy before the collision and compare it to the total kinetic energy after.
The formula for kinetic energy is $KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$.

**Initial Kinetic Energy (Before the jump):**
Only the runner is moving, so we only calculate their energy.
$$KE_{initial} = \frac{1}{2}m_1v_1^2$$
$$KE_{initial} = \frac{1}{2}(70)(3)^2$$
$$KE_{initial} = 35 \cdot 9$$
$$KE_{initial} = 315 \text{ Joules}$$

**Final Kinetic Energy (After the jump):**
Now the combined mass ($210 \text{ kg}$) is moving at the new speed of $1 \text{ m/s}$.
$$KE_{final} = \frac{1}{2}(m_{total})(v_{final})^2$$
$$KE_{final} = \frac{1}{2}(210)(1)^2$$
$$KE_{final} = 105 \cdot 1$$
$$KE_{final} = 105 \text{ Joules}$$

Since $315 \text{ J} \neq 105 \text{ J}$, a significant amount of kinetic energy ($210 \text{ Joules}$) was lost during the collision.

---

### 3. Final Answers

- **Final speed of the cart and runner:** $1 \text{ m/s}$
- **Is kinetic energy conserved?** No. 210 Joules of kinetic energy are lost in the collision (converted to heat, sound, or impact deformation), which is characteristic of an inelastic collision.
