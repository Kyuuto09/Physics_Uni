# Problem 6: Energy Dissipation

### 1. Problem Statement

A tennis ball is dropped from a height of 2.0 m. After each bounce, it loses 30% of its mechanical energy. To what height does it rise after the second bounce?

---

### 2. Solution and Explanation

**Concept Intuition:**
Gravitational Potential Energy ($PE$) is calculated using the formula $PE = mgh$.
Because the mass of the tennis ball ($m$) and Earth's gravity ($g$) never change, the potential energy is directly tied to the height ($h$).

If the ball loses 30% of its total mechanical energy upon hitting the floor, it means it retains exactly 70% of its energy for the next bounce. Because energy is directly proportional to height, retaining 70% of its energy means it will bounce to exactly 70% of its previous height.

#### Step 1: Find the height after the first bounce

The ball starts at an initial height of 2.0 m. It retains 70% (or 0.70) of that height after hitting the ground once.
$$h_1 = h_0 \cdot 0.70$$
$$h_1 = 2.0 \cdot 0.70$$
$$h_1 = 1.4 \text{ m}$$

_After the first bounce, the ball rises to 1.4 meters._

#### Step 2: Find the height after the second bounce

For the second bounce, the new "starting height" is 1.4 m. It hits the ground again and loses another 30% of its energy, retaining 70% of this new height.
$$h_2 = h_1 \cdot 0.70$$
$$h_2 = 1.4 \cdot 0.70$$
$$h_2 = 0.98 \text{ m}$$

_(Alternatively, you can calculate this all in one step by squaring the retained energy percentage: $h_2 = 2.0 \cdot (0.70)^2 = 0.98$ m)._

---

### 3. Final Answer

- **Height after the second bounce:** 0.98 m
