# Problem 1: Gauss's Law

### 1. Problem Statement

A point charge of $+2 \text{ C}$ is located at the origin. Calculate the electric flux through a spherical surface of radius $1 \text{ m}$ centered at the origin.

---

### 2. Solution and Explanation

**Concept Intuition:**
Gauss's Law is one of the most elegant and fundamental concepts in electromagnetism. It states that the total "flow" of electric field lines (the electric flux, $\Phi_E$) out of a perfectly closed 3D boundary is entirely determined by how much electric charge is trapped *inside* that boundary.

Think of the point charge like a glowing light bulb and the flux as the total amount of light radiating out. It doesn't matter if you put the bulb inside a small glass sphere or a giant glass box; the *total* amount of light passing through the glass into the outside world is exactly the same—it depends solely on the wattage of the bulb. 

Because of this, the radius of $1 \text{ m}$ given in the problem is actually a distraction! Whether the radius is $1 \text{ m}$, $10 \text{ m}$, or an irregularly shaped balloon, the total flux remains identically the same as long as the $+2 \text{ C}$ charge is enclosed.

#### The Calculation

The mathematical formula for Gauss's Law is simply:
$$\Phi_E = \frac{q_{enc}}{\varepsilon_0}$$

Where:
- $\Phi_E$ is the electric flux.
- $q_{enc}$ is the total enclosed charge.
- $\varepsilon_0$ is the vacuum permittivity constant, which is approximately $8.854 \times 10^{-12} \text{ C}^2 / (\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2)$.

We simply plug in our enclosed charge of $2 \text{ C}$:
$$\Phi_E = \frac{2}{8.854 \times 10^{-12}}$$

$$\Phi_E \approx 2.259 \times 10^{11} \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2 / \text{C}$$

---

### 3. Final Answer

- **Electric Flux ($\Phi_E$):** $\approx 2.259 \times 10^{11} \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2 / \text{C}$
- **Key Takeaway:** The $1 \text{ m}$ radius is irrelevant to the final answer. The total flux depends exclusively on the enclosed charge.
