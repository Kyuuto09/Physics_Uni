# Problem 10: Speed of Light in Media

### 1. Problem Statement

What is the speed of light in a diamond, which has an index of refraction $n = 2.42$?

---

### 2. Solution and Explanation

**Concept Intuition:**
We often hear that "the speed of light is a constant," but that is only strictly true in a perfect vacuum! When light enters a physical, transparent medium (like water, glass, or diamond), it interacts with the atoms in that material and slows down.

The **Index of Refraction ($n$)** is simply a ratio that tells you exactly how much the light is being slowed down compared to its maximum speed in a vacuum. Because light can never travel faster than it does in a vacuum, $n$ is always $1.0$ or greater. 

Diamond has an incredibly high index of refraction ($2.42$), meaning it is optically very dense. This extreme slowing and bending of light is exactly what gives diamonds their famous "sparkle" and brilliance!

#### Step 1: Identify Known Variables
- Index of refraction for diamond ($n$): $2.42$
- Speed of light in a vacuum ($c$): $\approx 3.0 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$

#### Step 2: Set Up the Equation
The formula defining the index of refraction is:
$$n = \frac{c}{v}$$
Where $v$ is the velocity (speed) of light in the specific material. 

We can rearrange this equation to solve for $v$:
$$v = \frac{c}{n}$$

#### Step 3: Calculation
Substitute our known values into the rearranged equation:
$$v = \frac{3.0 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}}{2.42}$$
$$v \approx 1.23966 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$$
$$v \approx 1.24 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$$

---

### 3. Final Answer

- **Speed of light in diamond ($v$):** $\approx 1.24 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$
