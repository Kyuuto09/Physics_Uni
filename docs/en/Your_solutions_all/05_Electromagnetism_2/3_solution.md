# Problem 3: Biot-Savart Law

### 1. Problem Statement

A small segment of a line wire of length $0.1 \text{ m}$ carries a current of $3 \text{ A}$. The segment is located at a distance of $0.2 \text{ m}$ from a point $P$. Calculate the magnetic field at point $P$ due to this current segment (assume the segment is perpendicular to the line connecting it to point $P$).

---

### 2. Solution and Explanation

**Concept Intuition:**
The Biot-Savart Law is used to find the magnetic field generated by a small, specific piece of a wire (a current element). Unlike Ampere's Law, which is best for infinitely long wires or highly symmetric setups, the Biot-Savart Law allows us to calculate the contribution of just a tiny segment $\Delta l$. 

The magnetic field $\Delta B$ produced by a small segment is directly proportional to the current and the length of the segment, and inversely proportional to the square of the distance. It is also highly dependent on the angle between the wire and the point of interest.

Because the problem explicitly refers to a "small segment" and specifies it is exactly perpendicular to the line connecting it to $P$, this is a classic setup intended for the differential approximation form of the Biot-Savart Law.

#### Step 1: Identify Known Variables
- Length of the segment: $\Delta l = 0.1 \text{ m}$
- Current: $I = 3 \text{ A}$
- Distance to point $P$: $r = 0.2 \text{ m}$
- Angle between the segment and the line to $P$: $\theta = 90^\circ$ (since it is perpendicular)
- Magnetic constant: $\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} = 10^{-7} \text{ T}\cdot\text{m/A}$

#### Step 2: The Biot-Savart Law Formula
The magnitude of the magnetic field from a small segment is given by:
$$\Delta B = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{I \cdot \Delta l \cdot \sin(\theta)}{r^2}$$

#### Step 3: Calculation
First, we evaluate the angle component. Since the segment is perpendicular to the line connecting it to $P$, the angle is $90^\circ$.
$$\sin(90^\circ) = 1$$

Now, substitute the rest of the values into the equation:
$$\Delta B = (10^{-7}) \frac{3 \cdot 0.1 \cdot 1}{(0.2)^2}$$
$$\Delta B = (10^{-7}) \frac{0.3}{0.04}$$
$$\Delta B = (10^{-7}) \cdot 7.5$$
$$\Delta B = 7.5 \times 10^{-7} \text{ T}$$

---

### 3. Rigorous Extension: Finite Wire Approach

*(Note: While textbook problems using the phrase "small segment" expect the approximation above, a rigorous physicist might note that $\Delta l = 0.1 \text{ m}$ isn't infinitely small compared to $r = 0.2 \text{ m}$. For completeness, here is what the exact calculation of a finite wire of $L = 0.1 \text{ m}$ yields at its perpendicular bisector)*

The exact formula for a finite wire is:
$$B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{4\pi r} 2\sin(\theta_{end})$$
Where $\theta_{end}$ is the angle to the ends of the wire. We find it using a right triangle with adjacent side $r = 0.2$ and opposite side $L/2 = 0.05$:
$$\sin(\theta_{end}) = \frac{0.05}{\sqrt{0.05^2 + 0.2^2}} = \frac{0.05}{\sqrt{0.0025 + 0.04}} = \frac{0.05}{\sqrt{0.0425}} \approx 0.2425$$

Substituting this into the finite wire formula:
$$B = (10^{-7}) \frac{3}{0.2} \cdot 2 \cdot 0.2425 = (10^{-7}) \cdot 15 \cdot 0.485 = 7.275 \times 10^{-7} \text{ T}$$

As you can see, the "small segment" approximation ($7.5 \times 10^{-7} \text{ T}$) is very close to the exact finite integration ($7.275 \times 10^{-7} \text{ T}$), validating the intended use of the standard Biot-Savart approximation.

---

### 4. Final Answer

- **Magnetic Field ($B$):** $7.5 \times 10^{-7} \text{ T}$ (or $0.75 \, \mu\text{T}$) based on the standard small-segment Biot-Savart approximation.
