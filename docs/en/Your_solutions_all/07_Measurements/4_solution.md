# Problem 4: Relative Uncertainty (Speedometer)

### 1. Problem Statement

A car's speedometer has a 5% of uncertainty. If it reads 60 km/h, what is the range of the car's actual speed?

---

### 2. Solution and Explanation

**Concept Intuition:**
Uncertainty can be expressed in two main ways:
1.  **Absolute Uncertainty:** A specific number of units (e.g., $\pm 3\text{ km/h}$).
2.  **Relative (or Percentage) Uncertainty:** A fraction or percentage of the total reading (e.g., $\pm 5\%$).

To find the range of the actual speed, we first need to convert the percentage uncertainty into an absolute uncertainty, and then add and subtract it from the measured value.

#### Step 1: Calculate the Absolute Uncertainty
The problem states the relative uncertainty is 5% of the speedometer's reading.
$$\text{Absolute Uncertainty } (\Delta v) = 5\% \text{ of } 60\text{ km/h}$$
$$\Delta v = 0.05 \cdot 60\text{ km/h}$$
$$\Delta v = 3\text{ km/h}$$

This means the speedometer could be off by exactly $3\text{ km/h}$ in either direction.
So, our measurement is $v = (60 \pm 3)\text{ km/h}$.

#### Step 2: Determine the Range
The "range" is simply the lowest possible speed up to the highest possible speed.
*   **Minimum possible speed:** $60 - 3 = 57\text{ km/h}$
*   **Maximum possible speed:** $60 + 3 = 63\text{ km/h}$

---

### 3. Final Answer

*   **Range of actual speed:** $57\text{ km/h}$ to $63\text{ km/h}$
