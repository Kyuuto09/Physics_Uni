# Problem 5: Percentage Calculation

### 1. Problem Statement

A measurement of time is recorded as $t = 5.45 \pm 0.22\text{ seconds}$. What is the percentage uncertainty of this measurement?

---

### 2. Solution and Explanation

**Concept Intuition:**
This problem asks us to do the exact opposite of Problem 4. We are given the **absolute uncertainty** ($\pm 0.22\text{ s}$), and we need to figure out what fraction or percentage that error is compared to the entire measurement ($5.45\text{ s}$). 

Percentage uncertainty is very useful because it gives us an immediate sense of how "good" a measurement is, regardless of the units. (Being off by $0.22\text{ seconds}$ is terrible if you are measuring a $1\text{-second}$ sprint, but it is incredibly precise if you are measuring a $10\text{-year}$ process!)

#### Step 1: Set up the Percentage Formula
The formula to convert an absolute uncertainty ($\Delta t$) into a percentage uncertainty is simply to divide the error by the measured value ($t$) and multiply by $100\%$:
$$\text{Percentage Uncertainty} = \left( \frac{\Delta t}{t} \right) \cdot 100\%$$

#### Step 2: Calculate the Percentage
Substitute our given values ($t = 5.45\text{ s}$, $\Delta t = 0.22\text{ s}$):
$$\text{Percentage Uncertainty} = \left( \frac{0.22}{5.45} \right) \cdot 100\%$$
$$\text{Percentage Uncertainty} \approx (0.04036) \cdot 100\%$$
$$\text{Percentage Uncertainty} \approx 4.036\%$$

#### Step 3: Rounding the Final Answer
The absolute uncertainty we were given ($0.22$) has two significant figures. Therefore, we should round our percentage to two significant figures as well: $4.0\%$.

---

### 3. Final Answer

*   **Percentage Uncertainty:** $4.0\%$
