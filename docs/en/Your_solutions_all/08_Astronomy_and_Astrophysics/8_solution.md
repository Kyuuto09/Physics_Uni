# Problem 8: Interplanetary Travel

### 1. Problem Statement

How much time would it take to get from Earth to Mars when Mars is at its closest approach ($55,000,000\text{ km}$):
a) for a message sent at the speed of light?
b) by a spacecraft traveling at a constant speed of $40,000\text{ km/h}$?
c) by an "airplane" traveling at a constant speed of $900\text{ km/h}$ (typical for intercontinental flights)?

---

### 2. Solution and Explanation

**Concept Intuition:**
Because the distances in space are so unfathomably huge, standard formulas like $t = d/v$ (time equals distance divided by velocity) lead to some incredibly staggering results when we plug in everyday speeds!

#### Part A: Speed of Light
When mission control on Earth talks to rovers on Mars, they use radio waves, which travel at the speed of light ($c \approx 300,000\text{ km/s}$).
$$t = \frac{d}{c}$$
*(In words: Time is equal to the total distance divided by the speed of light).*

$$t = \frac{55,000,000\text{ km}}{300,000\text{ km/s}}$$
$$t \approx 183.3\text{ seconds}$$

If we divide by 60 to get minutes, that is just over **3 minutes**. This means a radio signal sent from Earth takes 3 minutes to reach Mars, and the reply takes 3 minutes to get back—a total of 6 minutes round-trip just to say "Hello!"

#### Part B: Fast Spacecraft
A spacecraft traveling at $40,000\text{ km/h}$ (which is roughly the speed of the Apollo spacecraft heading to the Moon). Let's keep our distance in kilometers so the units match!
$$t = \frac{d}{v}$$
*(In words: Time is equal to the total distance divided by the velocity of the spacecraft).*

$$t = \frac{55,000,000\text{ km}}{40,000\text{ km/h}}$$
$$t = 1,375\text{ hours}$$

To make this number more understandable, let's use dimensional analysis to convert hours into days:
$$1,375\text{ hours} \times \frac{1\text{ day}}{24\text{ hours}} \approx 57.3\text{ days}$$
So, traveling in a straight line at Apollo-era speeds, it would take just under **2 months**.

#### Part C: Boeing 747 Airplane
What if you could magically pave a highway through space and fly a standard commercial airplane at $900\text{ km/h}$?
$$t = \frac{d}{v}$$
$$t = \frac{55,000,000\text{ km}}{900\text{ km/h}}$$
$$t \approx 61,111\text{ hours}$$

Let's convert those massive hours into days, and then into years!
1.  **Hours to Days:**
    $$61,111\text{ hours} \times \frac{1\text{ day}}{24\text{ hours}} \approx 2,546\text{ days}$$
2.  **Days to Years:**
    $$2,546\text{ days} \times \frac{1\text{ year}}{365.25\text{ days}} \approx 6.97\text{ years}$$

It would take you almost **7 straight years** of flying 24/7 without stopping to reach Mars in a commercial jet!

---

### 3. Final Answer

*   **a) Speed of Light:** $\approx 183\text{ seconds}$ (or $\sim 3\text{ minutes}$)
*   **b) Spacecraft:** $1,375\text{ hours}$ (or $\approx 57\text{ days}$)
*   **c) Commercial Airplane:** $\approx 61,111\text{ hours}$ (or nearly $7\text{ years}$)
